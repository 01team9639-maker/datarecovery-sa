Common levels such as RAID 0, 1, 5, 6 and 10 can be assessed, as well as NAS, SAN and software RAID. The path follows the number of disks, how the data is distributed, and the controller and file system. Details on the RAID and servers page.

Ransomware questions

It may be possible depending on the ransomware family, the encryption pattern, the state of the backups, and whether deleted originals, snapshots or unencrypted fragments survive. No promise can be made before a sample from the case is analysed. More on the service.

Usually not. Removing the malware may stop further activity, but it does not decrypt files whose contents have already been changed. Cleaning the system and recovering the data are two separate jobs.

Paying does not guarantee a working key, and it does not prove that a stolen copy of the data was deleted. The decision can carry management, legal and regulatory dimensions, so it starts with a technical assessment of the backups, the data and the alternatives — not with the attacker's promise.

No. Some families have reliable tools in specific circumstances; others have no general decryptor. Files can also be partially encrypted or damaged. The family, the version and the encryption pattern must be identified before choosing a path.

Keep multiple backups, with one immutable or disconnected from the network, and test restores regularly. Close exposed remote access, enable multi-factor authentication, patch systems, reduce account privileges, and separate the backup network from production. Details on the ransomware page.

Isolate it from the network by pulling the cable or disabling the port, and disconnect backup units before the encryption reaches them. Do not delete files, do not format and do not rebuild. Record the time and the affected systems and coordinate with whoever owns security or IT.

Keep the ransom note, the new file extension, a sample of an encrypted file, and an intact copy of the same type if one exists. These help identify the family and the encryption method — though a matching extension alone is not always enough.

Database files such as MDF and LDF, and virtual machine images such as VMDK and VHDX, can be assessed, because some attacks encrypt scattered portions of large files. The structure and the intact pages are examined on a copy before any attempt to start the database or the virtual machine.
